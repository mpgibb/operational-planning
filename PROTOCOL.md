# Service capacity planning protocol

Decision: lock one site's next seven days of regular staffing at the start of the week. Each worker supplies 12 service units per day. The site has 8–26 regular workers daily, at most 135 regular worker-days weekly, and a maximum four-worker change between adjacent days within the week. There is no cross-week ramp constraint in this study. Regular cost is $120 per worker-day. After daily demand is observed, up to five overtime workers can be purchased at $180 each; remaining unmet requests incur a $24 penalty each and are lost, not backlogged. There is no inventory or staffing-induced demand.

Generate 156 weeks of original synthetic daily demand with weekday/annual seasonality, trend and autocorrelated weekly shocks. Final 52 weeks are a rolling evaluation. At each Monday cutoff, only completed earlier weeks enter forecasts or residual scenarios. One-week-ahead prediction uses a ridge trend/seasonal/recent-level model; calibration uses up to 52 archived one-week forecast errors formed before their outcomes occurred. Resample entire seven-day residual vectors, preserving within-week dependence. Marginal 80% demand intervals are descriptive under dependence and shift, not distribution-free coverage guarantees.

Policies: (1) a seasonal-naive 20% buffer rule, projected to the same constraints by minimizing squared deviations from target daily staffing; (2) optimization against the point forecast; (3) stochastic expected-cost optimization over residual scenarios; (4) a perfect-demand-information lower bound under the same staffing and recourse constraints. The last is an evaluator oracle, not deployable. Use exact dynamic programming over day, cumulative regular worker-days and prior-day staffing. Recourse evaluates every allowed overtime count. No arbitrary infeasible baseline is allowed.

Primary outcomes: realized total cost, fraction of demand served, shortage units, overtime worker-days and unused capacity; evaluate on held-out demand only. Include marginal forecast 80% coverage, WAPE, and a weekly paired four-week moving-block bootstrap interval for cost differences against the buffer rule (1,000 replicates). Fifty-two weeks is one synthetic history; the interval does not establish business savings. Cost sensitivity re-solves the stochastic plan at shortage penalties $12/$24/$48 using unchanged information and reports the service/cost tradeoff.

Stress scenarios: stable process; sustained 20% demand surge from the first held-out week; doubled future weekly/daily noise. Publish all results without favorable-seed or scenario selection. No labor-law, shift-length, skill-mix, absence or multisite feasibility claim is made; these would be required for a real staffing application.

Correctness: dynamic programming equals exhaustive enumeration on small fixtures; staffing bounds/budget/ramp constraints; exact overtime economics; oracle cannot cost more than any feasible policy; no future demand enters forecasts; fixed-seed reproducibility. Independent technical review remains pending.

Reference: Hyndman & Athanasopoulos, Forecasting: Principles and Practice, time-series cross-validation and prediction intervals. https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html and https://otexts.com/fpp3/prediction-intervals.html . The constrained staffing model and synthetic process are study-specific.

Numerical reproducibility note: forecast values are rounded to six decimal places before planning; expected daily costs are compared in integer micro-dollars. Equal objectives use the stable state-order tie break. This prevents machine-level rounding from selecting different equal-cost schedules. The cost discretization can change a seven-day objective by at most seven micro-dollars relative to the unrounded optimum. This implementation correction was made after an independent cross-platform reproduction check; seeds, holdouts, costs, scenarios and policy definitions were not tuned to outcomes.
